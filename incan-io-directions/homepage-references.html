A home for the language

The intended experience: a newcomer understands what Incan is, gets a feel for writing it, and finds a sensible first step. An existing user can reach documentation and project news quickly. The page should also feel like a place with its own identity.

This is a comparison of official homepage content and rendered openings, with a closer look at Oven’s explanation and workflow sections. It is a design interpretation, not a usability study or evidence of conversion performance. No visual direction is being selected here.

What Oven adds

Your Oven Alpha page has a purposeful progression: an operating idea, the problem behind it, normal commands, an architecture explanation, inspectable evidence, and explicit Alpha boundaries. Readers can follow the story or enter through section links.

Incus is a baker tending an oven, and the Loaf illustration represents the artifact being explained. Those images participate in the explanation. That is a stronger use of the identity than inserting a mascot next to an unrelated claim.

I would borrow its sense of place, its coherent explanation, and its progressive depth. I would simplify the introductory language: compatibility closures and receipts are suitable for an Oven explanation, but they would be too much before a visitor understands Incan. Its generated-Rust discussion also describes the existing Alpha context; it should not become the permanent Incan homepage story while 0.6 changes the compilation model.

The conclusion for our outline

There is no single programming-language homepage formula. Several of these sites use slogans, benefit sections, illustrations, and proof of adoption. Those elements are not inherently wrong. Our earlier attempts failed because the page did not establish a useful encounter with the language before asking the visitor to accept abstract claims.

My strongest content references are Gleam for an approachable technical introduction, Elixir for source and explanation together, Zig for concrete design choices, and Oven for a coherent story and identity. Ruby and Python contribute the sense of a welcoming project home; Kotlin, Rust, Go, Swift, and Julia contribute practical routes, examples, and project resources.

The proposed order is: meet Incan → read Incan → try Incan → understand its choices → explore real projects → discover its tools → follow its development. Essential content remains visible in a scrollable document. No autoplay, compulsory tabs, or invented adoption evidence.
